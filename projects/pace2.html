P.A.C.E. 2.0

Short Description

P.A.C.E. 2.0 is a second-generation Portable Automation and Control Environment developed for PLC experiments, instrumentation, and engineering labs. It trades some of the original system’s portability for expanded I/O, an integrated HMI and network switch, multiple protected power rails, and improved access for maintenance and modification.

Project Overview

The original P.A.C.E. platform was designed to pack as many useful industrial controls as possible into the smallest practical enclosure. Although functional, its dense arrangement made wiring, servicing, and future expansion more difficult than I wanted. Rather than continue working around the physical constraints, I developed P.A.C.E. 2.0 as a larger, deeper, more capable second-generation platform.

The redesigned unit can interface with external Allen-Bradley PLCs through two DB-25 connectors and has provisions for an internal PLC. It brings together operator controls, indicator lamps, digital and analog signals, an HMI, Ethernet connectivity, and multiple DC supply rails in one movable laboratory platform.

From the outset, this iteration placed greater emphasis on electrical protection, organized industrial wiring, grounding, troubleshooting, and integration with existing BYU–Idaho feedback-control equipment. The completed core functions have already proved useful in laboratory work, even as the internal PLC and AC output subsystems remain under development.

Evolution from P.A.C.E. 1.0

P.A.C.E. 1.0: maximize operator I/O and portability within a compact enclosure. This succeeded as a portable control interface, but created tight spacing, difficult internal access, and limited room for additions.

P.A.C.E. 2.0: prioritize versatility, serviceability, protection, and expansion. A larger enclosure accommodates dedicated DIN-rail assemblies, organized wiring ducts, multiple supplies, network equipment, additional lamps, an integrated display, and future AC functions.

The objective was not simply to build a bigger control panel; it was to build a more useful and maintainable laboratory platform based on the practical lessons of the first version.

Features

  • Keyed mains control and separate AC-enable switch.
  • Fully integrated and operational emergency stop acting on electrical control power and control logic.
  • Hand-Off-Auto selection with dedicated Start and Stop buttons.
  • Six digital-input toggle switches, expanded indicator lamps, an electromechanical counter, and an analog potentiometer.
  • Dual DB-25 interfaces for external PLC connections.
  • Designed interface capacity of 10 digital inputs, 10 digital outputs, 8 analog inputs, and 8 analog outputs.
  • Integrated touchscreen HMI and internal Ethernet switch with external network connection.
  • Dedicated DB-9 auxiliary interface for compatible feedback-control laboratory equipment.
  • Operational regulated 24 VDC, 12 VDC, and 5 VDC supply rails, with approximately 5–6 A class supplies.
  • Internal mains breakers, protected DC branches, power monitoring, and dedicated protective grounding.
  • Standardized color-coded 14, 16, and 18 AWG MTW conductors sized for circuit loads.
  • Organized industrial terminal blocks, relay interfaces, and wiring ducts.
  • Installed hardware for future switched AC outputs and internal PLC operation (not yet commissioned).

Control, I/O, and Networking

Operator Controls and External PLC Interface

P.A.C.E. 2.0 retains the tactile operator controls of the original platform while adding more indicator capacity and a broader set of analog and digital connections. Two DB-25 connectors are used for external PLC interfacing, supporting the design’s planned 10-in/10-out digital and 8-in/8-out analog capabilities.

HMI and Network Integration

The built-in touchscreen HMI provides a second operator interface, and an internal Ethernet switch supports communication between networked components. An external Ethernet connection makes it easier to integrate the platform with other laboratory and industrial control systems.

Laboratory Auxiliary Interface

A separate DB-9 auxiliary port simplifies connection to compatible feedback-control lab equipment, reducing repeated ad hoc wiring when moving between experiments.

Internal PLC — Future Integration

The enclosure is designed to host an internal PLC so the unit can eventually operate independently of an external controller. Final internal PLC wiring and configuration are still outstanding.

Power Distribution and Safety

Operational DC Power

The system provides 24 VDC, 12 VDC, and 5 VDC rails for different control and instrumentation needs. Power distribution includes branch protection, monitoring, and organized grounding and return paths to improve usability and troubleshooting.

Emergency Stop and Protective Devices

The emergency-stop system is fully integrated, tested in use, and operational on both electrical and control-logic levels. Internal breakers, fusing, and protective grounding are built into the design rather than being deferred to a future revision.

AC Expansion — Not Yet Commissioned

Two AC output receptacles are installed, with future provisions for four outputs. The design also considers split-phase 120/240 V operation with a center-tapped neutral. The internal AC circuits, relay wiring, and AC outputs are not yet wired and commissioned; these remain future work rather than operational features.

Wiring Standards

All cabinet conductors use standardized Machine Tool Wire (MTW) in 14, 16, or 18 AWG, selected according to the connected equipment and circuit loads. A consistent color scheme makes circuits easier to trace and corresponds with my other industrial panel work.

  • Black: AC line
  • White: AC neutral
  • Green: protective earth
  • Red: +24 VDC
  • Gray: DC common / return
  • Purple: relay control
  • Blue: 24 V digital I/O
  • Yellow: analog I/O

Wiring is routed through organized ducts and industrial terminals to make testing, modifications, and future service work more accessible than in the compact first-generation design.

Technical Challenges

  • Design tradeoffs: choosing a larger enclosure to gain space for maintenance, expansion, and power distribution without sacrificing all portability.
  • Mixed-signal integration: organizing analog and digital I/O around power distribution and relay control while maintaining usable signal references.
  • Safety architecture: integrating the emergency stop into both electrical and logic behavior, alongside protective devices and grounding.
  • Multiple supply rails: distributing 24, 12, and 5 V power with appropriate conductor sizing, protection, and monitoring.
  • Operator experience: balancing physical controls, status lamps, a counter, and the HMI without making the panel difficult to interpret.
  • Interoperability: supporting external PLC wiring, Ethernet communication, and a dedicated interface for existing laboratory equipment.
  • Serviceability: using standardized MTW, consistent colors, organized terminals, and wiring ducts to simplify future changes.

Development Status

P.A.C.E. 2.0 began in November 2026 and is approximately 80% complete. Its core functions are operational, including the HMI, external PLC interfaces, network connectivity, DC supplies, operator controls, and fully integrated emergency stop. It has already been used with Allen-Bradley equipment in BYU–Idaho laboratories.

Remaining Work

  • Internal AC circuits and outputs: finish wiring, configuration, inspection, and commissioning.
  • Internal PLC integration: complete wiring and configuration for standalone control.

The remaining work is limited to those two subsystems; the other core functions described above are already in service.

Miscellaneous Notes

P.A.C.E. 2.0 demonstrates an important lesson from its predecessor: the smallest enclosure is not necessarily the best engineering solution. The additional space makes the platform much easier to inspect, maintain, adapt, and extend.

The project also complements my Industrial PLC Trainer 3.0 work: both emphasize industrially recognizable components, organized wiring, protection, documentation, and educational usability.
